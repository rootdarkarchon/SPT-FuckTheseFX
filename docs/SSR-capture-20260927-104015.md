## Interpretation and next decision

The user's report of little visible difference is consistent with the final composites. Both modes intentionally retain the same native resolve, history, roughness mips and material composition. This preserves the established overall response even when ray records differ. GGX also approaches mirror reflection as material smoothness approaches one; actual receiver smoothness is not exported here, so that explanation cannot be confirmed specifically for this water.

The captured hit-record difference is real, but one frame per mode cannot separate stochastic sampling from animated normal changes or establish convergence and temporal stability. These captures also contain no GPU timing comparison. They do not demonstrate a perceptual benefit that justifies preferring StochasticNative.

Keep HiZNative as the working reference and StochasticNative as an optional experiment. Prioritize cost/quality evaluation and optimization of the working native-downstream path before expanding the AMD denoiser integration. A future stochastic quality comparison should include rough reflective materials, where its intended benefit is more apparent; no repeat of this seven-phase riverside capture is needed to establish the findings above.
